AFoundations: Variables, Scales & Frequency TablesQ1–Q4
1
A logistics company records four variables per shipment: delivery time (hours), route type (urban / suburban / rural), number of packages, and customer satisfaction rating (1–5 stars). Which variable is measured on an ordinal scale?
Customer rating (1–5 stars) is ordinal: ranked categories with no guarantee of equal spacing between values. Delivery time and packages are ratio variables (true zero, equal intervals). Route type is nominal (unordered categories).
2
A frequency table has class [40,…50) with cumulative relative frequency $F_3 = 0.72$. A manager states: “72% of values fall within the interval [40,…50).” What is wrong?
The cumulative relative frequency $F_i = \sum_{j=1}^{i} f_j$ accumulates all proportions up to and including class $i$. Saying '72% fall in [40,50)' confuses $f_i$ (class-specific frequency) with $F_i$ (the running total up to that class).
3
A dataset of 300 employee salaries shows $\bar{x} = €48\,000$, median $= €39\,000$, mode $= €35\,000$. The HR director reports: “the typical salary is €48,000.” What is the most precise critique?
The sequence mean > median > mode is the signature of right (positive) skewness. A small number of very high earners pulls $\bar{x}$ above the value experienced by most employees. The median (€39,000) is the robust, appropriate summary for skewed salary data.
4
Two investment funds have identical standard deviations $\sigma = 9\%$. Fund A has mean annual return $\bar{x}_A = 3\%$; Fund B has $\bar{x}_B = 14\%$. What does the coefficient of variation (CV) reveal?
$CV = \sigma/\bar{x}$ normalises dispersion by the mean. Equal $\sigma$ does not mean equal relative risk: Fund A's $CV = 3.0$ implies three times more risk per unit of return than Fund B's $CV = 0.64$. This is the key insight CV adds beyond raw standard deviation.
BDispersion, Shape, Concentration & OutliersQ5–Q7
5
A distribution of stock returns has skewness $AS_1 = +1.9$ and excess kurtosis $K = +3.1$. Which description is correct?
$AS_1 = +1.9 > 0$ → long right tail. $K = +3.1 > 0$ → leptokurtic (excess kurtosis above the normal’s baseline of 0). For financial returns this combination is common: extreme positive events occur more often than a normal model predicts.
6
Country X has Gini index $= 0.26$; Country Y has Gini index $= 0.53$. Both experienced the same nominal GDP growth over the last decade. Which conclusion is most justified?
The Gini index (0 = perfect equality, 1 = maximum concentration) measures distributional inequality, not aggregate wealth. Country Y’s higher Gini means growth has been concentrated in fewer hands — a core distinction between GDP-level and distributional analysis.
7
MULTI — select ALL correct A dataset of 400 monthly sales figures contains 15 outliers identified by the $1.5 \times IQR$ rule. Which statements are correct?
✓ (a) Outliers inflate $\sigma$ by increasing squared deviations from the mean. ✓ (c) The mean is not resistant to extreme values; the median shifts only marginally. ✗ (b) The median is a resistant statistic — it barely changes when extremes are removed. ✗ (d) Outliers must be investigated (data entry error? legitimate extreme?), never automatically deleted.
CVisualization & Bivariate DistributionsQ8–Q10
8
A bar chart compares satisfaction scores for four hotels: 96.2%, 96.9%, 97.5%, 98.1%. The Y-axis starts at 95.8% instead of 0. What is the primary statistical problem?
Darrell Huff's 'gee-whiz graph' (How to Lie with Statistics, 1954): starting the Y-axis at 95.8% transforms a ~2% absolute spread into a visually dramatic gap. Correct approach: start at 0, or use a clearly labelled inset with disclosed truncation.
9
In a bivariate table of Gender (M / F) × Purchase Decision (Yes / No), the analyst computes $P(\text{Yes} \mid \text{Female}) = 0.73$. A manager says: “73% of all buyers are women.” Is this correct?
The inverse fallacy: $P(A\mid B) \neq P(B\mid A)$ in general. By Bayes' theorem, $P(F\mid Y) = P(Y\mid F)\cdot P(F)/P(Y)$, which can differ substantially from $P(Y\mid F)$.
10
Two variables — subscription tier (Basic / Premium / Enterprise) and churn (Yes / No) — are cross-tabulated. The conditional distributions of churn are identical across all tiers. What does this imply?
Statistical independence: $P(A\mid B_k) = P(A)$ for all categories $B_k$. If conditional churn distributions are identical across tiers, tier carries zero information about churn — by definition, the variables are independent.
DMeasures of AssociationQ11–Q14
11
A researcher studies the association between job satisfaction (Likert 1–7) and annual salary (€), where salary shows significant right skewness. Which measure of association is most appropriate?
Pearson's $r$ assumes linearity and is sensitive to non-normality and outliers. When salary is skewed, the rank-based Spearman's $\rho$ is more robust. Cramér's $V$ and $\phi$ apply to categorical × categorical associations only.
12
A $\chi^2$ test on a $3 \times 4$ contingency table yields $\chi^2 = 19.8$, $p = 0.019$. What is the correct interpretation?
$p = 0.019 < 0.05$ → reject $H_0$. Two key nuances: (i) $\chi^2$ does not measure strength of association — use Cramér's $V$; (ii) statistical association is not causation.
13
In a cohort study, the Relative Risk (RR) for cardiovascular disease (smokers vs non-smokers) is 2.1. In a case-control study of the same population, the Odds Ratio (OR) is 3.4. What best explains the discrepancy?
OR approximates RR only at low outcome prevalence. Formally: $OR = [p_1/(1-p_1)] \div [p_0/(1-p_0)]$. When $p$ is substantial, the $(1-p)$ denominators diverge from 1, inflating OR above RR.
14
MULTI — select ALL correct Which statements about Pearson’s $r$ are correct?
✓ (b) $r$ measures linear association only. ✓ (d) $r^2$ quantifies explained variance. ✗ (a) $r=0$ means no linear relationship — perfect non-linear dependence (e.g., $Y = X^2$ centred at 0) gives $r=0$ yet $Y$ is fully determined by $X$. ✗ (c) It is $r^2 = 0.72$ that captures 72% of explained variance, not $r = 0.85$.
ERates of Variation, CAGR & DeflationQ15–Q18
15
A firm’s revenue was €80 M in 2019 and €124 M in 2023. Analyst A reports “55% growth.” Analyst B computes CAGR $= (124/80)^{1/4} - 1 \approx 11.6\%$. Which statement is true?
$(124-80)/80 = 55\%$ is the fixed-base cumulative rate. CAGR $= (124/80)^{1/4}-1 \approx 11.6\%$ compounds to the same total: $(1.116)^4 \approx 1.55$. The 55% describes total growth; CAGR enables year-over-year comparisons across different time horizons.
16
A country’s nominal GDP grew 9.2% last year. The GDP deflator rose by 5.7%. What was the approximate real GDP growth?
Linear approximation: real $\approx 9.2\%-5.7\%=3.5\%$. Exact: $(1.092/1.057)-1 \approx 3.3\%$. Deflation separates genuine productivity gains from pure price-level changes.
17
An economist converts household income data (2000–2023) to constant 2010 euros. What is the primary analytical purpose?
Deflation converts a nominal (current-price) series to a real (constant-price) series. A salary that doubled from 2000 to 2023 represents little real gain if prices also doubled. Only deflated series support conclusions about real living standards.
18
MULTI — select ALL correct Which statements about fixed-base rates versus chain rates are correct?
✓ (a) Fixed-base anchors every comparison to period 0. ✓ (c) Cumulative growth $= \prod_{t=1}^T(1+g_t)-1 \neq \sum g_t$. ✓ (d) A low base year amplifies subsequent rates visually; a high base compresses them — Huff’s ‘base-year trick.’ ✗ (b) Chain and fixed-base rates differ numerically except in trivial (uniform growth) cases.
FIndex Numbers: Laspeyres, Paasche & FisherQ19–Q23
19
A Laspeyres price index (base 2020 = 100) for 2024 equals 119.3; the Paasche index equals 115.8. What does the pattern $L > P$ indicate?
The general result $L \geq F \geq P$ holds when prices and quantities move inversely (consumers substitute away from expensive goods). Laspeyres overweights goods that became expensive because it ignores substitution. Fisher's $F = \sqrt{L \cdot P}$ splits the difference.
20
Fisher’s ideal price index: $F = \sqrt{L \times P}$. Given $L = 122$, $P = 117$, compute $F$ and explain why Fisher is called ‘ideal’.
Irving Fisher (1922): the geometric mean of $L$ and $P$ satisfies two key axiomatic tests: (1) time reversal $F_{0,t}\cdot F_{t,0}=1$; (2) factor reversal $F_\text{price}\times F_\text{qty}=$ value index. This is why the US BEA and IMF recommend it for price statistics.
21
Eurostat and most statistical agencies use Laspeyres-type formulas for the CPI. What is the main practical reason?
The operational advantage is decisive: current household expenditure survey data (needed for Paasche/Fisher weights) are available only with a lag of months or years; the CPI must be published monthly. Base weights are updated every 5 years to limit accumulated substitution bias.
22
A price index reads 143.7 in period $t$ (base period $0 = 100$). Which interpretation is correct?
$I_t = (P_t/P_0)\times 100 = 143.7 \Rightarrow P_t = 1.437\cdot P_0$ — a 43.7% increase. Common errors: (a) treating the index as an absolute unit increment; (b) treating it as a multiplicative factor of 143.7×.
23
MULTI — select ALL correct Which statements correctly describe the substitution bias of the Laspeyres CPI?
✓ (b) Fixed base-period weights overweight goods whose relative prices rose. ✓ (c) Direction of bias: upward (Laspeyres overestimates true cost increase). ✗ (a) Laspeyres uses base-period (not current) quantities. ✗ (d) Fisher minimises bias by averaging $L$ (upward bias) and $P$ (downward bias).
GTime Series: Decomposition & ForecastingQ24–Q29
24
A retail chain’s monthly sales series shows: (i) a long-run upward trend, (ii) regular spikes every November–December, and (iii) erratic month-to-month fluctuations. Which standard decomposition component is absent from this description?
Classical decomposition has four components: Trend ($T$), Cyclical ($C$), Seasonal ($S$), Irregular ($I$). Annual Christmas spikes = $S$; long-run growth = $T$; erratic noise = $I$. Missing: the cyclical component ($C$) — long-wave business-cycle fluctuations of 3–10 years, not fixed-period.
25
Applying a 12-month centred moving average to a monthly time series eliminates which component(s)?
A 12-month centred moving average averages exactly one full calendar year at each point, cancelling the 12-month seasonal pattern and smoothing most short-term irregular noise. What remains is the slow-moving trend-cycle $T_t\cdot C_t$ — the structural backbone of the series.
26
Using an additive decomposition model, the trend projects €3.1 M for Q2. The seasonal component for Q2 is $-0.20$ M; the cyclical component is $+0.08$ M. What is the point forecast?
Additive model: $\hat{Y}_t = T_t + S_t + C_t$ (irregular $I_t$ is not forecast). $3.1 + (-0.20) + 0.08 = 2.98$. The negative seasonal component for Q2 reflects a below-average quarter, partially offset by a positive cyclical reading.
27
A time series model gives a 95% CI of [280, 320] for the conditional mean and a 95% prediction interval of [251, 349] for a future individual observation. Why is the prediction interval wider?
$\text{SE}^2_\text{pred} = \text{SE}^2_\text{mean} + \hat{\sigma}^2$. The extra $\hat{\sigma}^2$ is the irreducible natural scatter of individual observations. Even with infinite data ($\text{SE}_\text{mean}\to 0$), the PI width remains $\approx 2\times 1.96\hat{\sigma}$.
28
MULTI — select ALL correct Which statements about autocorrelation in time series regression are correct?
✓ (a) Definition of positive first-order autocorrelation: $\text{Cov}(\varepsilon_t,\varepsilon_{t-1})>0$. ✓ (b) OLS remains unbiased but loses efficiency; SEs are biased (usually underestimated), inflating $t$-statistics. ✓ (d) Standard remedies. ✗ (c) Durbin-Watson tests for first-order serial correlation in residuals, not unit roots — use the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test for unit roots.
29
Simple exponential smoothing: $\hat{Y}_{t+1} = \alpha Y_t + (1-\alpha)\hat{Y}_t$, where $\alpha \in (0,1)$. Which statement correctly compares $\alpha = 0.05$ versus $\alpha = 0.95$?
The weight on observation $k$ periods ago is $\alpha(1-\alpha)^k$. With $\alpha=0.95$, weights collapse to near zero within 2–3 periods — the model tracks recent changes closely but amplifies noise. With $\alpha=0.05$, weights decline very slowly — a long memory, stable but slow to respond to genuine structural shifts.
HSimple Linear RegressionQ30–Q34
30
OLS regression of advertising spend $X$ (€000s) on monthly sales $Y$ (€000s) yields $\hat{Y} = 115 + 4.2X$. A manager says: “each extra €1,000 in advertising generates exactly €4,200 in sales.” Which critique is most complete?
Three critical nuances: (1) average — individual predictions carry uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}$; (2) valid range — extrapolation beyond the data is unreliable; (3) no causation — seasonality, competitor campaigns, or a third variable could drive both simultaneously.
31
A simple regression yields $R^2 = 0.68$. Which interpretation is most precise?
$R^2 = 1 - SS_\text{res}/SS_\text{tot}$ is the proportion of variance in $Y$ explained by the model. It says nothing about causation, significance of individual coefficients, or whether individual data points lie on the line.
32
After fitting a regression, the residual plot (residuals vs. $\hat{Y}$) shows a clear fan shape — the spread of residuals increases as fitted values grow. What does this indicate?
Heteroscedasticity violates a key OLS condition. Estimates remain unbiased but lose efficiency; standard errors are biased (usually underestimated), inflating $t$-statistics. Remedies: WLS, log-transformation of $Y$, or heteroscedasticity-consistent (HC) standard errors.
33
MULTI — select ALL correct Which statements about Cook’s distance in regression diagnostics are correct?
✓ (b) Cook’s $D_i \propto \sum_j(\hat{Y}_j^{(-i)}-\hat{Y}_j)^2$: the total shift in fitted values when observation $i$ is deleted. ✓ (c) Influence = leverage × residual size: both must combine for high influence. ✗ (a) VIF measures multicollinearity, not Cook’s $D$. ✗ (d) Cook’s $D$ is defined for any regression, including simple.
34
In simple linear regression with one predictor, what is the algebraic relationship between the global $F$-statistic and the $t$-statistic for $\hat{\beta}_1$?
With $k=1$ predictor: $F = t^2$ exactly (follows from $F_{1,\nu} = t_\nu^2$). Both test $H_0:\beta_1=0$. The $F$-test becomes a distinct, more powerful test only in multiple regression, where it tests whether all $k$ slopes are simultaneously zero.
IMultiple Linear Regression & Model DiagnosticsQ35–Q40
35
A multiple regression model is $\hat{Y} = 42 + 3.1X_1 + 0.9X_2 - 1.8X_3$. How is $\hat{\beta}_1 = 3.1$ correctly interpreted?
The defining feature of multiple regression coefficients is the ceteris paribus interpretation: $\hat{\beta}_1$ isolates the marginal effect of $X_1$ after statistically controlling for all other predictors in the model — hence the term ‘partial slope.’
36
A multiple regression has VIF values: $X_1 = 1.3$, $X_2 = 9.4$, $X_3 = 9.8$. What is the most likely consequence?
$\text{VIF}_j = 1/(1-R_j^2)$ where $R_j^2$ measures how well $X_j$ is predicted by the other regressors. $\text{VIF}>5$–10 signals severe multicollinearity: inflated SEs, unstable coefficients, yet potentially high $R^2$ — a classic multicollinearity paradox.
37
In a multiple regression, the global $F$-test is significant ($p < 0.001$) but all individual $t$-tests are non-significant ($p > 0.10$). What is the most likely explanation?
This combination is a classic diagnostic fingerprint of multicollinearity. The $F$-test evaluates whether the full set of predictors collectively explains more variance than chance — it can be highly significant even when every individual $t$-test is not, because those $t$-tests are penalised by inflated standard errors.
38
When a new predictor is added to a model, $R^2$ always increases (or stays the same), but adjusted $\bar{R}^2$ decreases. What does this mean?
Adjusted $\bar{R}^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_\text{res}/(n-k-1)}{SS_\text{tot}/(n-1)}$ penalises each added predictor. It rises only if the new predictor reduces $SS_\text{res}$ by more than the penalty warrants. A decrease signals the variable adds noise rather than signal. AIC and BIC provide similar penalised criteria.
39
MULTI — select ALL correct Which of the following are fundamental conditions that OLS relies on to produce unbiased estimates with valid standard errors?
✓ (a) Zero mean errors ensure unbiasedness. ✓ (b) Homoscedasticity ensures efficiency and valid SEs. ✓ (d) No autocorrelation ensures efficiency and valid SEs. ✗ (c) Normality of predictors is not required. Normality of residuals is needed only for exact finite-sample $t$/$F$ inference — and relaxes asymptotically via the Central Limit Theorem.
40
MULTI — select ALL correct Which statements correctly describe the risk of overfitting in multiple regression?
✓ (b) Bias-variance trade-off: adding parameters reduces training error but increases prediction variance on new data. ✓ (c) Penalised criteria trade off fit against parsimony. ✗ (a) Overfitting is characterised by high training $R^2$ combined with poor out-of-sample performance. ✗ (d) Irrelevant predictors add noise, inflate SEs, and overfit.
